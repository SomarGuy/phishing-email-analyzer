    Why I built this

    From Diogo Ramos · computer science student, Toronto

    Before I went back to school for computer science, I worked as an advisor at Geek Squad. A lot of the laptops that came across the counter had the same story attached. Someone got an email that looked like it was from their bank, or a courier, or “Microsoft”, clicked once, and then we were the ones cleaning it up.

    What stuck with me is that these people were not careless. The emails were just good enough, and nobody had ever shown them where to look. I kept thinking I wished I could hand them something that opens the email for them, touches nothing, and says in plain words why it is worried.

    So that is what this is. It reads the headers, the route the email took to get to you, whether the sender passed the usual sign-in checks (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), where each link really goes, what each attachment really is, and how the writing tries to rush you. Then it adds it up. The part I care about most is that every single point in the score has a reason sitting next to it, so you can look at the number and disagree with it.

    What it can’t do

    I would rather say this up front than have you find it. Every email in this mailbox is one I wrote, and I also wrote the checks, so my 34 out of 36 is me marking my own homework. I wrote a second batch after I froze the weights to be a bit fairer to myself, and it still found a miss (the one with the RAR file). It also wrongly flags a security awareness email that quotes a phishing example. It has never seen real mail.

    It does not truly verify DKIM signatures, it does not read QR codes, it does not look inside RAR files, and the wording checks only know English. Treat it as a way to learn how this stuff works, not as something to trust with your inbox.

    What I would do next

    First, get someone else to write the test emails, so I am not both the author and the grader. After that, try it on real mail that people give me permission to use, add real DKIM checking, read QR codes, and open RAR and 7z files the same careful way I already read ZIPs.

    Diogo

    How to use this

    about a minute

    This is a phishing email analyzer I wrote. It opens an email without touching anything in it, checks the headers, the sign-in results (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), the links and the attachments, and gives it a score from 0 to 100 with a reason for every point. I built it because, back when I worked at Geek Squad, a lot of the laptops on the counter had one convincing email behind them. Everyone in this mailbox is made up, and nothing is ever opened or fetched.

    Try this in 60 seconds

    1. Open the email from "PayPal Security", subject "Action required: unusual sign-in detected". It is near the top of the Inbox (the button opens it for you). Read the red bar, then "Why it got this score". Every line has points, a plain reason and the evidence, and the total is 100 out of 100, "likely phishing".

    2. Click "Sign-in" in the "Jump to" row. SPF is a fail, DKIM is none and DMARC is a fail. Those are three checks on whether the sender proved who they are, and this one proved nothing.

    3. Click "Route". "The trip this email took" lists every server the message passed through (the header hops), oldest first, with the time from each server's own header.

    4. Click "Links". The text says https://www.paypal.com/verify but the link really goes to paypa1-secure[.]test, with a number 1 where the l should be. Links are shown defanged (hxxps and [.]) and are never clickable.

    5. Compare it with a safe one. Click "Looks fine" on the left, then the email "This week: harbour dredging, new ferry timetable" from Harbour Gazette. It says "Nothing fired. That is the whole story." and scores 0 out of 100.

    6. Click "Check your own email" at the top left, pick "P01-paypal-lookalike-credential" under "Nothing handy? Load one of mine:" and press "Check it". Your browser runs the same checks and scores it on the spot. You can paste your own email text instead, and nothing is uploaded.

    Words you will see

    Phishing
    A fake message built to trick you into handing over a password, money or access.
    Headers
    The details at the top of every email that most people never see: who sent it, the route it took and which checks it passed.
    Header hops
    Each mail server the message passed through on its way to you. Every one adds a line with a time, so the route can be read back oldest first.
    SPF
    Checks that the server that sent the email is allowed to send for that domain.
    DKIM
    A digital signature that shows the email really came from the domain's owner and was not changed on the way. This tool reads the result the mail server recorded and does not check signatures itself.
    DMARC
    The domain's rule for what to do when SPF and DKIM do not line up with the From address you can see. A fail is a strong sign of spoofing.
    Spoofing
    Making an email look like it came from someone else.
    Look-alike domain
    A web address that is one character off a real one, like paypa1 with a one instead of an l.
    Display name
    The friendly name next to the address. Anyone can set it to "PayPal Security", so the address behind it matters more.
    Reply-To
    Where your reply will go. It can quietly differ from who the email says it is from.
    Defanged
    A link written as hxxps and [.] so it cannot be clicked by accident.
    IOC (indicator of compromise)
    A sender address, domain, link or file fingerprint that is worth blocking. They are listed under "Things to block".
    BEC (business email compromise)
    A scam where someone poses as a boss or a supplier to get money moved, often with no link or attachment at all.
    HTML smuggling
    An attachment that builds the harmful file inside your browser, so nothing obviously bad was ever attached.
    Score and verdict
    0 to 100. From 25 it is "Suspicious" and from 55 it is "Looks like phishing". Below that it is "Looks fine".
    MITRE ATT&CK
    A public catalogue of attacker techniques with IDs like T1566. Each message is tagged with the ones it matches.

    What this shows about my skills

    I can read an email's headers and explain SPF, DKIM and DMARC to someone who is not technical, which is what a help desk gets asked all the time. I turn a pile of signals into a score, a reason for each point and a ticket note someone else can act on, and I say where it falls short: it missed one email in my second test set and it wrongly flags an awareness email that quotes a phishing example.

    Check your own email

    runs in this tab, nothing is uploaded

    Paste a whole raw .eml, or just the header block, and press Check it. Your browser runs the same header, sign-in, link, attachment and wording checks as the Python version and scores the result. The SPF check only knows the made-up DNS records that ship with the demo, so real domains show “none”. Please do not paste anything private on a computer you share.

    Things to block

    Sender addresses, domains, links and file hashes from every message that scored suspicious or worse (security people call these IOCs). They are shown defanged, like hxxp and [.], so nothing turns into a clickable link. The downloads carry both versions. All of it is made up.
